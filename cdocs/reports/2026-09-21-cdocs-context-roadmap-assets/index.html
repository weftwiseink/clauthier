Cdocs context roadmap

BLUF. Over 7 days of weftwise work, ~96% of tokens were cache-read (re-sent context: 3.94B vs 21.6M output). Dispatched general-purpose subagents were 64.9% of the $3,152 spend, and the implementer alone was 48.3%, invisible in usage.db because it dispatches with a bare subagent_type.

Fixes fall on four axes: label roles at dispatch, persist orientation outside the context window (chat record + devlog v2), share retrieval across agents (cache + graphify), and cap per-agent context (cap-and-reseed), with cheap-tier routing in parallel. Cap-and-reseed hard-depends on persist and share.

Scope caveat. One 7-day window (2026-09-12 to 09-20), weftwise only, dominated by loro-repo propose/implement loops (top 2 of ~40 sessions ≈ 75% of read intake). The role mix is workload-dependent; implementer dominance is a hypothesis about an implement-heavy week, not a certified structure. Do not over-index on it: a re-measurement checkpoint is on the roadmap. Token counts for reads are approximations (chars ÷ 4).

4. Why re-reads happen

~4.7–5.0M whole-file Read tokens (~70% of whole-file-read volume) are re-reads. At true context granularity (one .jsonl = one window) the cause is structural.

5. Warm-implementer context (schematic)

A durable implementer re-bills its whole transcript as cache-read each turn. Measured: top implementer 924 turns, 18 rounds, per-round peak 155k → 966k, alone 11.3% of week cache-read; the 46 implementers are 54.8%; corr(rounds, carried context) = 0.89.

6. Architecture: which layer attacks which cost

Move long-lived context out of re-sent windows into durable documents plus cheap, scoped retrieval.

11. Open questions and risks

  • Chat-record capture. A UserPromptSubmit hook is the likely mechanism for append-on-send; needs confirmation that it fires for every input path and how it behaves in subagents. Verbatim history may carry sensitive content, so decide storage location, gitignore default and retention.
  • Record vs devlog overlap. [DEVLOG] warns against a third summarization layer. The chat record must stay orientation-only (what was asked and done) and defer decisions and evidence to the devlog.
  • Graphify status. A2, A1 and I2 all lean on it; the reports say “in progress”, not shipped. Treat as a dependency to verify before P2 scoping.
  • Sonnet overseer escalation. Untested. The spike must measure when it consults the advisor and routing/dispatch regressions, not only cost.
  • Cap cutoff. 0.2M is tentative and probably too low; unmeasurable until re-briefing is cheap. Sequence I1 → A2 → I2.
  • Single-window bias. Implementer dominance may not hold in proposal- or debug-heavy weeks. Re-measure before over-tuning.
  • Amplification is not per-source. The projected cache-read split by source assumes uniform amplification; it is illustrative only.
  • Intentional Fable usage. 7.3% Fable is a maintainer decision; tiering work must not “correct” it.
  • Estimates. Effort sizes here are proposals; Fable-5.1 cache-write pricing is an estimate in [SPEND]; read tokens are chars ÷ 4.
